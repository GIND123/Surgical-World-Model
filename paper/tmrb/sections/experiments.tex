\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\subsection{Datasets and protocol}
\textbf{SurgVU} (in-vivo, no kinematics). Models are trained on all usable 3.3\,s windows of the 123 training cases and selected on the 12 validation cases; we report results on a fixed set of \todo{2000} windows drawn evenly from the 20 test cases. Two latents (0.5\,s, five frames) are given as context and the model predicts the remaining 2.8\,s.

\textbf{Kinematic grounding and transfer.} To test whether learned actions correspond to robot motion we use datasets with synchronized kinematics: JIGSAWS \cite{jigsaws} (da Vinci, bench-top suturing, knot tying and needle passing) and the SutureBot, SRT-H porcine cholecystectomy and CRCD subsets of Open-H-Embodiment \cite{openh}. SAR-RARP50 \cite{sarrarp} (clinical prostatectomy) tests transfer from porcine laboratory to human surgery. We verify synchronization instead of assuming it: for every episode we cross-correlate a video motion signal that does not involve the ego-motion estimator (the mean change of the VAE latents) with total arm motion, globally and per 2-minute chunk. In the public conversion of CRCD, 3 of 18 episodes are offset by a constant 1.2\,s, which we correct, and 7 drift or never lock, which we exclude from every kinematics-based analysis; SutureBot is synchronized.

\subsection{Compared action interfaces}
All world models share the tokenizer, the dynamics transformer, the data and the training budget, and differ only in how actions enter:
(i) \emph{none}---unconditional prediction;
(ii) \emph{state}---robot-state tokens without learned actions;
(iii) \emph{monolithic}---one continuous latent action per transition with the same total dimension as three arm actions (24), Genie-style \cite{genie};
(iv) \emph{monolithic-VQ}---one of twelve discrete latent actions, as in SurgWM \cite{surgwm};
(v) \emph{slots}---four unsupervised factored latent actions obtained by slot attention \cite{slotattn,flam};
(vi) \method{}---per-arm latent actions; each with and without the explicit endoscope action. We also re-implement SurgWM \cite{surgwm} with its own tokenizer, latent action model and MaskGIT \cite{maskgit} dynamics, and compare with Cosmos-H-Surgical-Simulator \cite{openh} on the kinematic benchmarks.

\subsection{Metrics}
\textbf{Fidelity:} PSNR, SSIM \cite{ssim} and LPIPS \cite{lpips} per predicted frame against the real frames, and FVD \cite{fvd} with the I3D network of \cite{styleganv}. Copying the last context frame and the VAE reconstruction of the ground truth bound these metrics from below and above.
\textbf{Controllability:} $\Delta$PSNR, the loss in PSNR when actions are swapped with those of another clip \cite{genie,surgwm}, reported separately for endoscope and arm actions.
\textbf{Endoscope compliance:} for commanded endoscope motions, the error between the commanded and the realized similarity transform, measured on generated video with the same estimator.
\textbf{Arm locality:} when only arm $k$'s action is changed, the fraction of the resulting change in the generated frames that falls inside arm $k$'s region.
\textbf{Embodiment consistency:} on generated frames, an instrument detector re-trained without test cases checks that the instruments on the robot are present and no others appear (precision, recall, hallucination and disappearance rates over the horizon).
\textbf{Kinematic grounding:} the $R^2$ of ridge regression from frozen latent actions to the per-arm end-effector displacement of the same transition, on held-out trials.
Means are reported with 95\,\% bootstrap confidence intervals over test cases.

\subsection{Implementation}
The dynamics transformer of size B has 12 blocks of width 768 (152\,M parameters with the action interface); size S has 8 blocks of width 512 and size L 16 blocks of width 1024. Models are trained with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-4}$, 2k warm-up steps, cosine decay), batch size 32, bfloat16, an exponential moving average of weights (0.9995), $\lambda=1$, $\beta=10^{-4}$, for \todo{100k} steps on one NVIDIA RTX PRO 5000 (48\,GB). Sampling uses 20 Euler steps. The video VAE is Wan2.2's (frozen); among five tokenizers it gave the best reconstruction per latent number on validation clips (34.4\,dB PSNR, 0.019 LPIPS at $224\times320$).
