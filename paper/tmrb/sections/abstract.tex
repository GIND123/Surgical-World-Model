\begin{abstract}
Surgical world models, potential simulators and policy evaluators, need either scarce robot kinematics or one latent action that must explain endoscope motion, instruments and tissue at once. Yet a teleoperated robot logs its embodiment: which arm holds the endoscope and which instrument each other arm carries. We present \method{}, a latent diffusion world model whose actions mirror the robot: an explicit endoscope ego-motion action and a latent action per instrument arm, inferred from its instrument region and bound via the log. Trained on 476\,h of in-vivo da Vinci video without kinematics, it alone among interfaces compared at equal capacity keeps the endoscope and each instrument controllable while beating a copied frame. Frame-wide latent actions (monolithic, slots) predict \val{diff/S_global-S_arms/psnr}--\val{diff/S_slots-S_arms/psnr}\,dB better but realise only \val{ctl/S_slots/gain_pct}--\val{ctl/S_global/gain_pct}\,\% of a commanded endoscope move (\method{}: \val{ctl/S_arms/gain_pct}\,\%); the trade-off holds at three times the size (monolithic: \val{diff/B_global-B_arms/psnr}\,dB better, \val{ctl/B_global/gain_pct}\,\%; \method{}: \val{ctl/B_arms/gain_pct}\,\%). On SutureBot, linear probes recover an arm's translation from its action as well as from the monolithic one (R$^2$ \val{kin/suturebot/S_arms/pos}), less than from slots; initialising kinematics-conditioned models from \method{} adds \val{e3/srth/arms/0.1/vs_scratch}--\val{e3/suturebot/arms/0.1/vs_scratch}\,dB with 10\,\% of the episodes, like the monolithic model.
\end{abstract}
