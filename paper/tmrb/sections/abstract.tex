\begin{abstract}
World models that predict how the operative scene evolves under a robot's actions could serve surgical robotics as simulators, policy evaluators and data generators. Current surgical world models either require synchronized robot kinematics, which exist for only a few hundred hours of laboratory data, or learn a single latent action from unlabeled video that must explain endoscope motion, several instruments and tissue response at once. We observe that the embodiment of a teleoperated surgical robot is visible even when its kinematics are not: system logs record which arm carries the endoscope and which instrument is mounted on every other arm, and the console overlay shows which arms the surgeon drives. We present \method{}, a world model whose action space mirrors the robot: an explicit endoscope ego-motion action and one latent action per instrument arm, inferred by an inverse-dynamics model that only observes the arm's own instrument region, bound to arms through the robot log. A latent spatio-temporal diffusion transformer trained with rectified flow and per-frame noise levels predicts future endoscopic video from context frames and these actions. Trained on \todo{604}\,h of in-vivo da Vinci video without any kinematic labels, \method{} \todo{improves controllability and fidelity over monolithic and slot-based latent actions at equal capacity}; its per-arm actions \todo{linearly predict real instrument kinematics on held-out robots}; and pre-training on unlabeled video \todo{improves kinematics-conditioned prediction on public dVRK benchmarks}. We release code, processed annotations and weights.
\end{abstract}
