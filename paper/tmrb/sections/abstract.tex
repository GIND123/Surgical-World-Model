\begin{abstract}
Surgical world models could serve as simulators, policy evaluators and data generators, but need either scarce robot kinematics or one latent action that must explain endoscope motion, several instruments and tissue at once. Yet a teleoperated robot logs its embodiment: which arm holds the endoscope and which instrument each other arm carries. We present \method{}, a latent diffusion world model whose actions mirror the robot: an explicit endoscope ego-motion action and a latent action per instrument arm, inferred from its instrument region and bound via the log. Trained on 476\,h of in-vivo da Vinci video without kinematics, it alone among interfaces compared at equal capacity keeps the endoscope and each instrument controllable while beating a copied frame. Frame-wide latent actions (monolithic, slots) predict \val{diff/S_global-S_arms/psnr}--\val{diff/S_slots-S_arms/psnr}\,dB better but realise only \val{ctl/S_slots/gain_pct}--\val{ctl/S_global/gain_pct}\,\% of a commanded endoscope move (\method{}: \val{ctl/S_arms/gain_pct}\,\%); at three times the size, \method{} comes within \val{diff/S_global-B_arms/psnr}\,dB of the smaller monolithic model and realises \val{ctl/B_arms/gain_pct}\,\%. On three public kinematic datasets, its arm actions linearly explain \val{kin/mean/S_arms/pos} of the end-effector translation variance (monolithic: \val{kin/mean/S_global/pos}); initialising kinematics-conditioned models from \method{} changes PSNR by \val{e3/suturebot/arms/0.1/vs_scratch} and \val{e3/srth/arms/0.1/vs_scratch}\,dB with 10\,\% of the episodes.
\end{abstract}
