\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Surgical video generation}
Diffusion and transformer models now generate realistic endoscopic video. Unconditional or text-conditioned models include Endora \cite{endora}, SurGen \cite{surgen}, Bora \cite{bora}, Ophora \cite{ophora} and the hierarchy-aware HieraSurg \cite{hierasurg}; FLAIR learns action priors from optical flow and injects them into a frozen backbone \cite{flair}. Structure-conditioned models steer generation with action graphs \cite{visage}, optical flow and user trajectories \cite{surgsora}, sparse masks \cite{surgiflowvid}, tool-tip trajectories with tissue affordances \cite{saw} or multimodal control experts \cite{suruniworld}. These models produce convincing appearance, but their controls describe pixels rather than the robot, and expert assessment shows that visual plausibility does not imply plausible instrument operation or tissue response \cite{surgveo}.

\subsection{Surgical world models}
Kinematics-conditioned world models predict endoscopic video from robot actions. Cosmos-H-Surgical fine-tunes a physical-AI foundation model and uses an inverse-dynamics model to label synthetic video for policy learning \cite{cosmoshsurg}; Open-H-Embodiment provides the largest open corpus of medical robot video with kinematics and an action-conditioned simulator trained on it \cite{openh}, later distilled for real-time use \cite{cosmoshdreams} and applied to policy evaluation \cite{cosmossurgdvrk}. KVLR lifts articulated kinematics to image-aligned control maps \cite{kvlr}, SurgCast predicts future instrument skeletons \cite{surgcast}, and world-action models jointly predict video and robot actions \cite{surgwam,endowam,jointvt}. Other work targets long-horizon drift \cite{surgvista}, hierarchical event structure \cite{s2hwm}, failure detection \cite{fomofd} and motion-centric benchmarks \cite{surgwmbench}. All of these need kinematics for training or control. The surgical vision world model of Koju et al.\ \cite{surgwm} is the closest action-free approach: it applies the Genie recipe \cite{genie} to SurgToolLoc video at $120\times180$ pixels and 1\,fps with twelve discrete latent actions.

\subsection{Latent actions and factorization}
Latent action models infer actions from unlabeled video by inverse dynamics and train a forward model on them \cite{genie,lapa,adaworld,villax}. Nikulin et al.\ show that such latents collapse when distractors correlate with the true actions and that a little action supervision during latent-action learning restores them \cite{laom}; object-centric decomposition \cite{oclam} and factored latent actions with one action per learned scene factor \cite{flam} reduce the problem in multi-entity scenes. Factoring out camera ego-motion makes motion targets comparable across moving and static cameras \cite{egowam}, and EndoWorld-JEPA separates dominant displacement from residual motion for endoscopic representation learning \cite{endoworldjepa}. \method{} differs in that its factors are not discovered but defined by the robot: one explicit endoscope action and one latent per instrument arm, bound to arms by the robot's own logs.

\subsection{Diffusion world models}
Our dynamics model follows diffusion and flow-matching transformers \cite{dit,pixartalpha,rectflow,flowmatching,sd3} trained with per-frame noise levels as in Diffusion Forcing \cite{dforcing}, which supports autoregressive rollout; related designs underlie game and robot world models \cite{diamond,unisim,cosmos,cosmospredict25} and video foundation models \cite{wan}.
