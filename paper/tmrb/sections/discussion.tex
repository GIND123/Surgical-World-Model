\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
Grounding the action space in the robot trades a little fidelity for control. Of the interfaces compared at equal capacity, data and budget, \method{} is the only one that both beats a copied frame and realises most of a commanded endoscope move, and its arm actions act on their own instrument more than any frame-wide action does (Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto}). The frame-wide actions predict \val{diff/S_global-S_arms/psnr}--\val{diff/S_slots-S_arms/psnr}\,dB better under action replay, where an action inferred from the real future can carry how that future looks, but they take over the endoscope motion: their explicit endoscope action is left with little to do. Replay fidelity alone therefore overstates what a controllable simulator delivers, and we report it together with commanded control, locality and consistency with the robot log. Model size can pay the cost of grounding: at size B, \method{} predicts as well as the monolithic latent action of size S and realises more of a commanded endoscope move than at size S. Whether the frame-wide actions gain as much from size is untested.

The fidelity gap sits where the arm actions cannot act. A mounted instrument has an image region in \val{bind/steps}\,\% of the latent steps; in windows without a bound instrument \method{} falls below the copied frame, and in \val{bind/n/none_undetected} of these \val{bind/n/none} windows no mounted instrument is detected at all, so holding boxes across detector gaps cannot reach them. Detector recall, occlusion, instruments outside the field of view and stale intervals in the system logs all limit the binding; a detector trained on more surgical data, or tracking through occlusion, would extend the reach of the arm actions. Zooms are realised at about half of their commanded magnitude, and commands beyond the range of real endoscope motion less than in proportion. Rollouts beyond the 2.8\,s training horizon drift for every interface, losing about half of the real instruments within 14\,s; training on longer windows, or on its own rollouts, would be needed for long simulations.

At size S, \method{} beats copying the last context frame by only \val{diff/S_arms-copy_last/psnr}\,dB PSNR and is worse in LPIPS: a copied frame is hard to beat in the first \val{horizon/trail}\,s, and a sampled future draws its own fine texture, which LPIPS penalises. At size B the margin grows to \val{diff/B_arms-copy_last/psnr}\,dB, and LPIPS differs from the copied frame's by \val{diff/B_arms-copy_last/lpips} \val{diff/B_arms-copy_last/lpips_ci}, now in \method{}'s favour. Linear probes show how much kinematic information the actions carry, not whether they suffice to control a robot \cite{lamstudy}; kinematics-conditioned prediction tests the latter without inferring actions from the real future.

SurgVU shows porcine training-laboratory procedures on da Vinci Xi systems; SAR-RARP50 tests transfer to human prostatectomy, but other procedures, platforms and pathologies remain untested. The model predicts appearance, not tissue mechanics: it gives no guarantee that a predicted tissue response is physically plausible, and an expert assessment of its predictions is still to come. Like other world models, it is meant for training, policy evaluation and data generation offline, not to guide a real procedure. Paired intervals over test cases capture the variation between cases but not between training runs, which further seeds of the main interfaces measure.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \method{}, a surgical world model whose action space mirrors the teleoperated robot: an explicit endoscope action measured from the video and one latent action per instrument arm, inferred from that arm's instrument region and bound to the arm through the robot's system logs, learned from 476\,h of in-vivo da Vinci video without kinematics. Compared with other action interfaces at equal capacity, data and budget, it is the only one that keeps the endoscope and each instrument controllable while predicting better than a copied frame; frame-wide latent actions predict better under replay but take over the endoscope. At three times the size, \method{} predicts as well as the smaller monolithic model and controls the endoscope better than at the smaller size. Grounding makes the arm actions local, the binding through the robot log decides which instrument an action moves, a forward decoder keeps the actions in use, and the console overlay adds nothing beyond the log. Signals that come with every recording of a teleoperated robot thus make action-free surgical world models controllable actuator by actuator.
